# AI evaluation, 3 November 2026 (E1 photos, E2 planted claims)

Model: Muse Spark Open on Fireworks (`accounts/fireworks/models/muse-spark-open`, zero data retention), the model the live server runs. Prompts `prompts/v4`, `damage-prompts/v3`, `consistency-prompts/v3`. Harness: `server/scripts/e1_eval.py --live --provider fireworks`, which sends every photo and claim through the production code (photo check before signing, damage reading, statement check, flags and lanes). The summary on System health comes from `server/scripts/e1_summary.py` (`server/src/evidence_server/reference/model_accuracy.json`).

## The set

72 photos and 6 claims with known answers, kept out of git in `data/e1/` (it holds public plates):

- **Public photos** from Wikimedia Commons under free licences (sources and licences in `data/e1/SOURCES.json`), labelled by eye before any model output was read: 11 readable plates (3 modern US plates, 8 antique California plates), 5 photos with no readable plate (blurred by the uploader, or no plate), 2 readable odometers, 3 photos that are not a car's odometer, 12 damage photos (crashes, fire, glass, a crushed roof, a missing bumper), 9 undamaged cars, 2 hail-damaged apples (a wrong subject).
- **Synthetic photos** with exact answers: 6 plates, 6 VIN labels (3 door-jamb stickers, 3 windshield-style plates), 4 digital odometers.
- **Hard copies**: 6 photos sent twice through messaging-app-like compression (1280 px, JPEG 55 then 50), 3 blurred, 3 darkened.
- **Held out from prompt tuning**: four public photos were the same as images used to tune the prompts on 30 October (a broken mirror and three odometers) and were removed. The 20 tuning photos are reported separately (2 November: 19/20 on both hosts).
- **E2**: 6 claims against the demo policy (2020 Honda Accord, plate DEMO101): 2 clean, 4 with one planted problem (another car's plate, another vehicle's VIN, a story about the rear while the photo shows the front, damage claimed on an undamaged car).

## Results

Rates are hits / n with a 95% Wilson interval. Every set is small: read the interval.

| What we checked | Result | 95% range |
|---|---|---|
| Reads the license plate exactly | 15 / 20 = 75% | 53% to 89% |
| Reads the VIN exactly | 3 / 6 = 50% | 19% to 81% |
| Reads the odometer exactly | 7 / 7 = 100% | 65% to 100% |
| Asks for another shot of a blurred, dark, wrong or unreadable photo | 25 / 25 = 100% | 87% to 100% |
| Asks for another shot of a good photo (lower is better) | 5 / 47 = 11% | 5% to 23% |
| Finds each damaged area a person marked | 21 / 21 = 100% | 85% to 100% |
| Names areas a person also marked, or the one next to it | 32 / 37 = 86% | 72% to 94% |
| Names the right kind of damage | 21 / 21 = 100% | 85% to 100% |
| Rates the severity the way a person did | 14 / 21 = 67% | 45% to 83% |
| Calls an undamaged car damaged (lower is better) | 0 / 9 = 0% | 0% to 30% |
| Raises the planted flag on a claim with a planted problem | 6 / 6 = 100% | 61% to 100% |
| Flags raised on those claims that were planted | 6 / 13 = 46% | 23% to 71% |

Cost and speed: 126 model calls, about $0.58 for the whole set with an empty cache; median call 10 s, 95th percentile 24 s.

## What went wrong (model)

- **Plates**: two antique plates read with a repeated digit; one antique embossed plate with no state name not recognised as a plate; one modern plate photographed from a distance refused as poor quality (the product asks for a closer shot, so the claimant would retake); the messaging-app copy of that photo read with one wrong letter. Every plate the model read on a modern synthetic or close-up plate was right.
- **VINs**: all 3 door-jamb stickers read exactly; all 3 windshield-style synthetic plates (light text on a dark strip, no car around it) refused as "not a VIN". When a VIN was read, it was right (3 / 3). The windshield-style images are ours and unrealistic; real windshield photos show the dashboard around the plate.
- **Damage**: no damaged area missed and no damage invented on an undamaged car, but extra areas named beside the right one (strict precision 57%, 86% when the neighbouring area counts), and severity one step off in a third of the cases.
- **Extra flags on the planted claims**: `PRIOR_DAMAGE_SUSPECTED` on the four claims that reuse one public photo of a car without its bumper cover (the model read that damage as old, which is defensible for that photo), and `STORY_UNCLEAR` / `DAMAGE_OUTSIDE_STATEMENT` on the glass claim. Every claim went to adjuster review; none was sent to SIU or fast track.

## What went wrong (our evaluation), fixed before the numbers above

The first run exposed four mistakes in how Claude built the set; each was a set-building bug, and no photo's answer was changed to agree with the model:

1. The synthetic VINs had 16 characters (an off-by-one in the generator); the model rightly refused them.
2. Every damage photo shared one claimed area ("Front bumper"), so the photo check rightly asked for a retake on windshield and roof photos; each damage photo now carries the areas a claimant with that photo would list.
3. Undamaged cars were labelled "no retake"; when a claim lists damage the photo does not show, asking for the damage photo is the product working, so they are labelled "retake".
4. The wrong-vehicle claim used a distant plate photo (rightly sent back for a closer shot before any match), and the wrong-VIN claim used one of the 16-character VINs; both now use valid close-ups. The undamaged-car claim now expects the code the product uses for it (`DAMAGE_PART_NOT_FOUND`).

## Limits

- Small sets: n is 6 to 47 per row; intervals are wide.
- Public photos may be in the model's training data, which can flatter the reads. They test reading, not live capture.
- No claim photos from a real phone through our link yet; plates are mostly antique or synthetic; VINs are synthetic.
- This is not a fraud-detection rate. It measures reading and checking; a fraudster who beats live capture (for example with a virtual camera) is outside what this set can show.

## Rerun

```bash
cd server && set -a && . ../.env && set +a
uv run python scripts/e1_eval.py ../data/e1/labels.json --live --provider fireworks --run-id <id>-labels
uv run python scripts/e1_eval.py ../data/e1/claims.json --live --provider fireworks --run-id <id>-claims

```

Answers are cached in the database (`model_calls`), so a rerun with unchanged prompts and photos costs nothing.
